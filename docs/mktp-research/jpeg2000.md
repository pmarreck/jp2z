# JPEG 2000 (J2K/J2C codestream and JP2 wrapper): MKTP research record

Retraceable account of how far jp2z's strict validation reaches toward
Maximum Known Technically Possible (MKTP) corruption detection, as the
term is defined in Validate's `TERMINOLOGY.md` B4: the payload is consumed
to its declared end, every known objective invariant is enforced, the
residual blind class is documented and the evidence is replayed against an
oracle the author did not write. This record indexes evidence; it does not
grant an MKTP ledger row. A passing mutation percentage is not proof of
completeness, and a listed reach is never evidence of corruption.

Entries are dated and appended; earlier results stay as they were
measured, labelled stale when superseded.

## 1. Scope and identity (2026-10-09)

- Format: ITU-T T.800 / ISO/IEC 15444-1 Part 1 codestreams (`.j2k`,
  `.j2c`) and the JP2 file format of Annex I (`.jp2`). Part 2 (JPX,
  arbitrary decompositions, Rsiz bit 15) and Part 15 (HTJ2K, Rsiz bit 14
  or cblksty bit 6) are recognised and reported as valid-but-unverified
  (finding 145), never decoded, never failed for being unsupported.
- Variants covered end to end: 5/3 reversible and 9/7 irreversible
  wavelets; all five progression orders; POC (main and tile-part);
  tile-parts (TPsot/TNsot, interleaved tiles); COD/COC/QCD/QCC/RGN main
  and first-tile-part overrides; precincts; SOP/EPH; PPM/PPT packed
  headers; TLM, PLM, PLT length cross-checks; code-block styles BYPASS,
  RESET, TERMALL, VSC, PTERM, SEGSYM; 1 to 16384 components with
  per-component precision, signedness and sub-sampling; JP2 boxes
  signature, ftyp, jp2h (ihdr, bpcc, colr, pclr, cmap, cdef), jp2c.
- Source identity: `yolo` at 54917d6 for every indexed experiment; 3953e2a
  adds the B.3 check recorded in sections 3 and 4.4. Section 4.5
  (2026-10-10) was measured on the tree that commits it, the first commit
  after 54531e2. Binary: `zig-out/bin/jp2z` built from that
  tree with Zig 0.16.0 (ReleaseFast default; `MUTE_DEBUG_STATUS` has no
  effect on a release build).
- Oracle: OpenJPEG 2.5.4 (`opj_decompress`, `opj_dump`, the C library
  behind `src/ffi/openjpeg_wrapper.zig`), from the project's Nix flake.
  Fixture corpus: `openjpeg-data` at 39524bd (flake input), 57 ISO
  15444-4 conformance files under `input/conformance` and 226 files under
  `input/nonregression`. In-repo copies of 26 conformance fixtures plus
  one real-encoder JP2 are hashed in `jpeg2000-fixtures.sha256`.
- Consumers: jpegz pins jp2z 54917d6 (`build.zig.zon`); Validate reaches
  jp2z through jpegz and lists `jpeg2000` as witnessed `full` on its 10
  labelled-good files with no MKTP variant row.

### Hostile-input safety

Every structural failure above returns before geometry is used; 16-bit
and 32-bit arithmetic that could overflow on legal values is widened
(numTilesXY, tileRect, TLM/PLM varints). The in-core mutation matrix runs
in-process so a panic fails the suite. The 2026-09-16 and 09-18 probes
recorded no crash across 27 fixtures × 400 trials (section 4).

## 3. Research trail

- 2026-09-06: severity policy written down (LEARNINGS.md): undecodable
  values FAIL, reserved-but-ignorable WARN, defined-but-unimplemented 145.
  POC REpoc/CEpoc bounds deliberately not checked (B.12.1.3 clamps).
- 2026-09-12: BDFN ruling, another decoder's tolerance never downgrades a
  proven nonconformance. Applied to TPsot ≥ TNsot (FAIL kept; Validate's
  downgrade request withdrawn 2026-09-19) and to COD Scod / cblksty
  reserved bits under Part 1.
- 2026-09-16: Annex E exponent relation. Hypothesis: E-10 ties reversible
  QCD exponents to precision and gain, so a mismatch is a FAIL. Rejected
  by the text (E.2 is informative) and by data (ISO p0_10, file1, file9
  exceed the equality). Landed as the inequality budget, WARN 260 only.
  Boundary: an 8→9 bit Ssiz change on p0_13 is undetectable by any
  codestream cross-check; 12-bit trips 260.
- 2026-09-16: absent-tile rule. Inflated Xsiz/Ysiz mutants declared tiles
  the stream never delivered; ISO b2_mono legitimately omits 9 of 25
  tiles because every omitted tile is empty in its 5×3 sub-sampled
  component (B.3/B.6). Rule: absent tile with samples FAIL, absent empty
  tile clean.
- 2026-10-09 (this entry): SIZ tile-origin constraint. Inventory of
  parseSizBody against Table A.9 found the grid guard checked
  XTOsiz ≤ XOsiz but not XTOsiz + XTsiz > XOsiz (B.3). Experiment 4.4
  below. Implemented red-first as FAIL 146 with the clause in the detail.
  Candidates found by the same inventory and not implemented tonight:
  ihdr-first and colr-required in jp2h (need the Annex I text; the
  test helpers build colr-less JP2s and would all have to change), MCT
  with fewer than 3 or geometrically mismatched components (needs G.2
  text; decode already refuses), COD-family markers in a non-first
  tile-part header (needs the Table A.1 placement text). Each is a
  recognised-but-unverified reach today, not evidence of corruption.

### 4.4 SIZ tile-origin constraint (2026-10-09, this record)

Purpose: does jp2z name a SIZ whose first tile ends at or before the
image origin, and does OpenJPEG agree?

Fixture: in-repo `c1_mono.j2c` (sha256
7ed01bd12e2123579b3177200162c9f6b684dc9ee92585cc4f89d2a7cd9e9b50,
303×179, one tile at (0,0)). Mutants by direct byte write (stream offset
16 = XOsiz, 24 = XTsiz): `illegal_50` XOsiz 100, XTsiz 50 (sha256
f2f23d612a7c3b29adfc52c3d7e9fd8608c3570988b6567f54ef7bc7bb8e3752);
`illegal_eq` XOsiz 100, XTsiz 100 (boundary, equality is illegal; sha256
a0beab1fa9d0c243eefec6217a3b14e0094b9d7a5bdd742df4eb422e9de00113);
`legal_offset` XOsiz 100, XTsiz 303 (legal grid; sha256
73e7700a1633a973ba4ca1d7c43487c6f1a6fcbf81cac57973717b11936e37bd).

Commands: `jp2z validate --strict FILE` (binary built from 54917d6, then
from the fix), `opj_decompress -i FILE -o OUT.pgm` (OpenJPEG 2.5.4).

Before the fix: pristine exit 0 (info 208, 254). `illegal_50` FAIL on
downstream symptoms only (5 of 7 tiles absent, numbps=0 with passes,
over/under-read), `illegal_eq` FAIL (3 of 4 tiles absent), no finding
named the SIZ. OpenJPEG rejected both at the SIZ marker: "Error with SIZ
marker: illegal tile offset". `legal_offset` decodes in OpenJPEG; jp2z
reports 251/252 because moving the image origin on a real codestream
changes the packet geometry the data was encoded for (a confound of the
mutation, not a disagreement about the SIZ rule).

After the fix: `illegal_50` and `illegal_eq` FAIL 146 at the SIZ marker
with the B.3 detail; the 27 in-repo fixtures and the 57 conformance
files produce no such finding (0 false positives). Unit test "validate:
SIZ tile grid must reach the image origin" is a classifier over both
axes and both sides of the boundary (XTsiz/YTsiz ∈ {1, 2, 3, 6} on a 4×4
image at origin (2,2)) plus the c1_mono mutant and the pristine file; it
failed before the implementation (196 pass, 1 fail) and passes after.

Differential over `input/nonregression` (151 of 226 files matched by
extension j2k/j2c/jp2/jpx/jpc): OpenJPEG says "illegal tile offset" on
three files, `issue427-illegal-tile-offset.jp2` (sha256
369a21da770aa2da0b2cf6742087db4d5ba4e32a62f41f7ea2c6f038c060812f, 182
bytes), `edf_c2_1002767.jp2` and `edf_c2_101463.jp2`. jp2z fails all
three with 146 from the pre-existing SIZ guard: their XTOsiz exceeds XOsiz
(4294967292, 64 and 1024 against an origin of 0), the other half of the
B.3 pair, which jp2z already failed before this entry. Verdicts agree
on every file and the new rule adds no disagreement; no other file
trips either tool's tile-offset check.

### 4.5 MQ termination and the unconsumed-tail allowance (2026-10-10)

Trigger: a report from Validate (2026-10-10) on two private PDFs holding
1923 and 190 JPX streams. Every tile in every stream carries one more
tile-part than its TNsot declares (FAIL 141, the class of section 7);
with TNsot corrected, 241 streams still FAIL 252 under strict while
OpenJPEG decodes them, and the probed case left 3 unconsumed bytes in a
code-block. The question for this entry: can a conformant code-block
segment leave more than 2 bytes unconsumed in jp2z's metric? The metric
is `segment length - bp`, where `bp` indexes the last byte the decoder
loaded, so 1 means every byte was loaded and 0 means the decoder also
asked for a byte past the end.

Method: a test-only MQ encoder written from the Annex C flowcharts with
its own copy of Table C-2 (`tests/unit/mq_encoder.zig`), with the two
terminations the standard defines: the FLUSH procedure (SETBITS, two
BYTEOUTs, a final 0xFF discarded) and the predictable termination of
D.4.2 (12 - CT bits shifted out, the final byte kept unless 0xFF; the
procedure OpenJPEG implements as `opj_mqc_erterm_enc`). 4000 seeded
random decision streams per termination (seed 0x4d51464c55534821, 1 to
500 decisions each over the 19 EBCOT contexts, LPS rate drawn from
{0, 0.02, 0.1, 0.3, 0.5}), each decoded with jp2z's decoder.

Results: every decision of all 8000 streams round-trips. The unconsumed
tail is 0 in all 8000 (histogram {4000, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0} for each
termination); past-end synthesis is at most 2 for both. Neither
termination leaves an unconsumed byte: the decoder loads two bytes
beyond the FLUSH output before it stops, so N bytes appended past a
FLUSH measure as a tail of N - 1, and any unconsumed byte is the
encoder's addition, not its termination. These facts are pinned by the
tests "MQ round-trip, standard FLUSH" and "MQ round-trip, predictable
termination" in `tests/unit/validate.zig`.

End-to-end fixture: a 4×4 single-component reversible codestream whose
only code-block is the 13-decision cleanup pass of one positive
coefficient at (0,0) (RL 1, UNIFORM 0 0, SC0 0, ZC contexts 3, 0, 0, 5,
1, 0, 0, RL 0, RL 0), FLUSH output 2 bytes, packet header built bit for
bit (B.10 inclusion and zero-bitplane tag trees, Lblock comma code,
0xFF stuffing). jp2z decodes it to pixel (0,0) = 129 and 128 elsewhere,
byte-identical to OpenJPEG 2.5.4 through the FFI wrapper, with no
entropy finding under strict validation. Classifier over appended zero
bytes {0, 3, 4, 8} (tails {0, 2, 3, 7}): 0 and 3 accepted, 4 and 8 FAIL
252 under strict and WARN under lenient, detail "max N byte(s), cap 2".
Both tests failed before the detail change (the classifier on the detail
text) and pass after; the cap itself did not move.

OpenJPEG comparison: `opj_mqc_check_pterm` warns "PTERM check failure:
N remaining bytes" when `bp + 2 < end`, the same line as a cap of 2 in
the `bp` bookkeeping jp2z's decoder mirrors, applied only when cblksty
has PTERM; jp2z applies it to every segment.

Census (strict, with the new detail; artifact
`jpeg2000-under-read-census-2026-10-10.txt`): 0 of 57 conformance files
report 252. 29 of 226 nonregression files do; 26 of them also carry other
FAIL codes and tails from 15 to 30635 bytes (fuzzed or desynchronized
inputs, most of which OpenJPEG rejects), and 3 have exactly one
code-block at a tail of 3 with no other entropy finding:
`issue206_image-000.jp2`, `issue559-eci-090-CIELab.jp2` and
`issue559-eci-091-CIELab.jp2`. OpenJPEG decodes those three with
"Non conformant codestream TPsot==TNsot" and jp2z FAILs them 141 at
SOT+11, the same pairing Validate reports; their cblksty is 0x00, so
OpenJPEG runs no remaining-bytes check on them.

Outcome: the allowance stays at 2, now the named `underReadCap` in
`src/decode/reconstruct.zig` with this derivation in its doc comment;
the 252 detail carries the maximum tail, the cap and the code-block
style so a consumer can measure its own files. No pin, API or verdict
change on any corpus file. Whether bytes past the coder's termination
inside a code-block contribution are a violation is not settled by text
in hand (B.10.7 contribution lengths, D.4.1 and D.4.2 termination);
until it is, 252 keeps the project's byte-budget stance (strict FAIL,
lenient WARN), and every known real-world member of the 3-byte class
FAILs on TNsot regardless.

### 4.6 Termination text obtained; 252 split by cblksty, 261 added (2026-10-12)

Question (from 4.5): are bytes past the coder's termination inside a
code-block contribution a violation? Method: the T.800 (11/2015) PDF was
fetched from BDFN's reference store over SSH (same route as the Annex E
extracts; SHA-256 B1CA01EE...69E0, pdftotext -layout) and B.10.7, C.2.9,
C.3.4, D.4-D.4.3 and D.6 were extracted verbatim into
`docs/T800_CODEWORD_SEGMENT_TERMINATION_VERBATIM.md`. The PDF itself is
copyrighted and stays out of the repository.

Rulings from the text. D.4.2 (non-PTERM): "Any technique that places all
of the needed bytes ... need not backtrack ... is acceptable", so a surplus
after a non-predictable termination is conforming. D.4.2 (PTERM): the
procedure "shall be used" and the length "is simply the total number of
bytes pushed out", so any surplus is a violation. B.10.7: a contribution
"must not end in a 0xFF"; D.4.2: 0xFF "shall not be sent"; D.6 (raw): the
last byte "shall not be an 0xFF value". D.4.1: the decoder appends 0xFF
and "no more than two 0xFF bytes" are sufficient (consistent with the
over-read cap). The D.4 NOTE about marker codes is "should" (WARN at most).

Implementation (red-first; tests/unit/validate.zig): 252 without PTERM is
a WARN in both modes above a floor of 3 unread bytes; 252 under PTERM is
strict FAIL / lenient WARN on any measurable tail; new 261
cblk_contribution_ends_ff is strict FAIL / lenient WARN, classified over
appended-byte sets ({}, {FF}, {00 FF}, {FF 00}, {FF 00 FF 00}, {00 00 00
FF}) with the exact byte offset of the 0xFF in the detail. The non-PTERM
severity was first set to INFO (the strict reading of "FAIL = proven"),
which removed the project's main entropy-corruption detector: mutation-
matrix sensitivity fell from {15, 16, 21, 19, 22} to {4, 5, 17, 6, 17}
(codestream family; sniper, bolter, zero-fill, shotgun, nuke) and the CLI's
flipped-byte fixture exited 0 with a 641-byte tail reported as info. BDFN
resolved it by policy: this validator is deliberately stricter than
forgiving decoders because that is the only way to detect real
corruption, and "technically wrong but accepted by other decoders" is
WARN. The floor of 3 is corpus-calibrated: the largest tail from a
conforming producer is 3 (issue206, issue559-eci-090/091, validate's 241
streams), the smallest on damaged data is 15 (nonregression census;
tails 4..14 unobserved). Every detail string now states what is wrong,
the byte offset where it starts, and that other decoders tolerate it
while this project cannot (messaging rule, BDFN 2026-10-12).

Registry outcome (2026-10-13). The coordinator approved 261 as an
append-only assignment and retained 252 at its number, with two
qualifications adopted here. The non-PTERM WARN is a heuristic suspicion,
not a proven nonconformance: a corpus floor shows what observed encoders
do and cannot establish that every conforming encoder stays under 3 bytes,
so the public contract says so instead of claiming it universally. The
PTERM strict FAIL has to rest on a termination violation rather than on
decoder lookahead bookkeeping; it does, because the MQ round-trip test
pins a tail of 0 on genuine predictable-termination output and the ISO
fixture p0_02 (TERMALL + PTERM + SEGSYM) is asserted free of c252 and c261
by name under strict validation.

Classification update (2026-10-14). validate's unified classification

## 6. Residuals (observed survivors and candidates, not proofs)

JPEG 2000 Part 1 carries no checksum over packets or headers, so some
mutations yield codestreams no cross-check can distinguish from a
legitimately different encoding. The classes below are stated as what
was observed, with the fixtures and labels that witnessed them. An
oracle decoding a mutant shows the mutant is decodable, not that it is
strictly valid, and a class that holds survivors may still hold members
an objective invariant would reject. Each class is therefore a candidate
for adjudication until a clause or a derived invariant settles it.

- Entropy-coded bytes whose change keeps every segment inside its byte
  budget (over-read cap 12 bytes without PTERM, 3 with; SEGSYM and
  TERMALL streams reject more). Witness: 758 of the 776 oracle-labelled
  "changed" sniper/bolter survivors of the 2026-09-18 labelling sit in
  packet data (`conformance/probe/<fixture>/labels.ndjson`, 27 fixtures).
  Within this class, a tighter per-segment termination model could still
  reject some members; the over-read cap is corpus-calibrated (p0_08
  needs 10), not derived from the standard. The under-read side is
  coder-derived (4.5) and, since 4.6, a WARN heuristic above a floor of
  3 rather than a FAIL: D.4.2 permits the surplus, so a mutant whose tail
  stays at or below 3 is a survivor of this class by construction
  (e1_colr bolter, g3_colr shotgun in the matrix).
- Code-block contributions carrying 3 bytes past the coder's termination
  (tail 3): one producer family. Witnesses: `issue206_image-000.jp2`,
  `issue559-eci-090-CIELab.jp2`, `issue559-eci-091-CIELab.jp2` (one
  code-block each) and Validate's 2113 private JPX streams, every one of
  which also FAILs 141 on TNsot. Settled 2026-10-12 (4.6): conforming per
  D.4.2, so 252 is quiet at tail 3 and WARN above it; these files keep
  their 141 FAIL.
- QCD/QCC exponents and mantissas on 9/7 streams, and reversible
  exponents above the E-10 budget (the standard permits them). Witness:
  14 of the 18 header-byte "changed" survivors (p0_09, p1_07, pterm_test
  and others per the labels). Shrinking a reversible budget is WARN 260;
  the remaining directions have no known invariant.
- SIZ precision, signedness and sub-sampling on a raw codestream.
  Witness: the two Ssiz sign-bit survivors (p0_02 component 0 and one
  other, per the labels). The JP2 wrapper adds the ihdr/bpcc cross-check
  and is not in this class.
- Comment bodies and CRG values: no codestream invariant constrains their
  content. Witness: p1_04's 65 KB COM segment (120 inert labels, 57
  unreplayable shotgun windows).
- ICC profile bytes and palette entries: observed inert or changed
  survivors on file1, file9 and balloon_eciRGB_icc (1 pclr entry among
  the 18 header survivors). ICC structure (header length, tag table) and
  palette ranges against the declared depths are candidate checks not yet
  adjudicated; this class is not shown undetectable.
- Semantically equivalent encodings: empty-packet bit forms (g3/g4 PPM/
  PPT survivors), oversize tile grids that still mean one tile, a
  progression order change on a one-layer one-component stream. Observed
  inert; equivalence was confirmed by identical oracle output on those
  fixtures only.

Implementation reaches (ours, not the format's): Part 2 and HTJ2K
codestreams (145), the disputed and missing rows in section 2, the
decode refusal of tile-level COD/COC changes to decomposition or wavelet.
